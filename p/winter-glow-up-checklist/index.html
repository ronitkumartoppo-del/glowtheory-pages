How to feel your best in winter: a gentle glow up plan

Chase daylight

Short days affect energy for many people. Getting outside in the morning, opening curtains straight away and taking a lunchtime walk are simple ways to get more daylight. Put "morning light" on your checklist so it happens on purpose.

Care for skin and lips in dry air

Cold air outside and heating inside both dry out skin. Many people switch to a richer moisturizer, keep lip balm close and use a humidifier. Treat it as part of a cozy routine, not a chore. (For ongoing skin concerns, see a dermatologist.)

Keep moving, gently

Winter is a good time for movement that feels nice: walks, stretching, dancing in the kitchen, a home yoga video. Aim for consistent and pleasant rather than intense.

Eat warm, regular meals

Soups, stews and warm breakfasts make winter feel comforting. Planning a few go-to meals for busy weeks helps you eat well when energy is low. No calorie counting needed.

Plan rest before the holidays plan you

December fills up fast. Block rest days on your calendar first, then add events around them. Decide in advance what you will say yes to and what gets a kind "no, thank you".

Stay connected

Shorter days can make it easy to withdraw. Schedule small connections: a call with a friend, a cozy movie night, a walk with someone. Connection is as much a part of a winter glow up as skincare.
