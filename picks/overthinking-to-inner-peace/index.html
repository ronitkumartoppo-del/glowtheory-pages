How to Stop Overthinkingsmall habits for a quieter mind

If you're searching for how to stop overthinking, you're probably replaying a conversation or running through every "what if" right now. Same here, more often than I'd like. You don't have to fight your thoughts to feel calmer. These five little habits help me step back, and below them is a short ebook if you'd like more.

5 ways to quiet the loop

Pick one, try it for a week, and see what feels right for you.

  1. Set a 15-minute "worry window"

    Choose a time each day to think things through on purpose. When a worry pops up earlier, jot it down and tell yourself you'll look at it in your window. Often it feels smaller by then.

  2. Ground yourself with 5-4-3-2-1

    Notice five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch, two you can smell and one you can taste. It gently pulls your attention back into the room you're in.

  3. Ask: "Is this a problem or a puzzle?"

    A problem has a next step, so write it down and do it. A puzzle is something you can't solve tonight. Naming the difference makes it easier to put the puzzle down for now.

  4. Give decisions a deadline

    For small choices, give yourself two minutes. For bigger ones, pick a date. A deadline stops a decision from quietly taking over your whole week.

  5. Build a calm wind-down routine

    A short evening routine, like a screen-free cup of tea, a brain dump in your journal and a few slow breaths, gives your mind a clear signal that the day is done.
